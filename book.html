Poetry Collection

We Left a Window Open to the Sea

Winner of the 2026 Frederick Smock Poetry Prize from the Fleur-de-Lis Press at The Louisville Review. Visit award page ↗

Praise for the collection:

Stephanie Wrobel (Taylor)
"Luminous and moving, these poems navigate the territories between presence and absence, between inherited grief and new growth. Elder writes with exquisite care about motherhood and loss, creating space for readers to mourn and celebrate simultaneously."

Kimberly Grey
"Elder's debut collection moves deftly across geographies—from New York to Ireland to Germany and back—drawing upon vivid images of place and body, memory and fairy tale. Where the boundaries between mother and daughter, painting and body, fairytale and memory begin to dissolve."

Stephanie Honum
"These poems shimmer with the particular loneliness of translation between languages and selves. Elder captures the tender, bruised space between what is lost and what remains, between the daughter leaving and the mother staying behind."

Nicole Maxey
"A powerful exploration of displacement, home, and female lineage. Elder's work is both deeply personal and universally resonant, speaking to the ways we carry our mothers with us across continents and time."

Monica Tuma
"These gorgeous poems capture the complexities of translation, displacement and return—whether they are about shouting out to loved ones across oceans or excavating what remains in the spaces between words, languages, and bodies."

Ellen Elder.
